Usable quotes from perplexity

"Self-critical thinking recruits error and threat systems
An fMRI study of self-criticism found increased activity in:
Lateral and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.
Dorsal anterior cingulate cortex.
Posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal regions.
Striatal areas involved in action and learning.
In some studies, the amygdala and insula.
The lateral prefrontal and dorsal anterior cingulate findings are consistent with error processing, behavioral inhibition, and attempts to resolve a perceived mistake
"The inner critic may begin as an error-detection system, but it can become counterproductive when the brain treats a design problem as evidence of personal threat."

"Experimental research has tested whether self-critical thinking causes people to generalize from a specific failure to a negative view of the whole self. Participants exposed to self-critical tasks showed greater endorsement of broad negative self-beliefs, such as moving from “I performed badly on this task” to “I am generally incompetent.”
For your course, this is an important distinction:
    Adaptive error signal: “The tutorial is unclear.”
    Destructive self-judgment: “I am incapable of making games.”"
"The destructive element is not feedback or high standards. It is the added identity threat:
    “This feature needs work” becomes “I am not the kind of person who can finish.”"


"The important finding is that the body responded to thinking about the stressor, not just to the original stressor itself.
The event may be over, but the nervous system can keep receiving the message that the event is still happening if the mind repeatedly replays it."

"Rockliff et al. examined compassion-focused imagery using HRV and salivary cortisol. Some participants showed increased HRV and reduced cortisol, suggesting activation of a soothing physiological response. However, the effects were not uniform: people with high self-criticism or insecure attachment sometimes had difficulty benefiting from compassion imagery.
Compassion is not automatically relaxing. For some highly self-critical people, being kind to themselves initially feels unsafe, embarrassing, undeserved, or emotionally threatening."
