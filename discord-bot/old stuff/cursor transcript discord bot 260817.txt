## The core idea (this is what makes it work)

On Discord, **invite links are never truly secret**. Anyone who has one can forward it. That is fine for your free course.

For paid access, enforcement does not live in the link. It lives in **who has which role**:

```mermaid
flowchart TB
    subgraph server [Your Discord Server]
        welcome[#welcome - everyone]
        freeChannels[#free-course - FreeMember role]
        paidChannels[#paid-course - PaidMember role]
    end

    join[User joins via any invite link]
    freeSignup[Free signup on site]
    paidCheckout[Paid checkout]
    freeRole[Bot assigns FreeMember]
    paidRole[Bot assigns PaidMember only after verified payment + linked Discord account]

    join --> welcome
    freeSignup --> freeRole --> freeChannels
    paidCheckout --> paidRole --> paidChannels
```

Someone can share the server invite all they want. Without the **PaidMember** role, they still cannot see paid channels.

That is the standard pattern used by paid Discord communities. You are not fighting a losing battle on link secrecy.

### Paid course (not shareable)

If they only type `username#1234`, you will eventually assign the role to the wrong person or fail to match them.

**What “not shareable” actually means in practice:**
- They cannot copy a link that grants paid access to someone else
- Paid channels require the role tied to their Discord account
- If subscription ends, the bot removes the role (and optionally kicks them from paid channels)

## What a Discord course actually feels like

A student does not open a lesson player. They join a server and look at channels.

A typical Discord course week looks like this:

1. They get pinged in `#announcements`: “Week 2 is open.”
2. They click a pinned message with a video, a PDF, or a link to your site.
3. They type a reflection in `#week-2`.
4. You reply in chat, or hop on a voice call.
5. Progress is “I read the pins / I showed up,” not a checklist with completed lessons.

That works if the course is **cohort + conversation + live presence**. It is a poor fit if the course is **self-paced modules, interactive exercises, and a clear start-to-finish path**.

- Interactive workshops on your own site (Paint Your Problem, Voice in Your Head)

Putting *that* product entirely inside Discord would mean stuffing workshops into chat pins. Students would bounce between Discord and your site anyway, and Discord would add friction without adding structure.

| More students | What you change |
|---|---|

| 40 | You cannot reply to every post. You reply to some, pin the useful ones, run Q&A as the main “I see you.” |
